# Operations Forecast Tab

## What this tab is for
Forecast captures the forward view: expected demand, planned delivery, and key assumptions.
It is used in leadership and planning reviews to align operational commitments with likely future workload.

## Before you start
- Gather input from Sales/Commercial regarding upcoming pipeline and tender probabilities.
- Review current production capacity and known future constraints.

## Key components
- **Pipeline Demand:** Estimates of future work volumes that are not yet committed to the master schedule.
- **Planned Delivery:** Expected output rates over the upcoming quarters.
- **Key Assumptions:** Explicitly stated conditions that the forecast relies upon (e.g., "Assumes Supplier X recovers by Q3").

## The analytical process
1. **Review inputs:** Compare current pipeline estimates against historical actuals (Overhaul Trends) for realism.
2. **Assess capacity impact:** Model how the forecasted demand will affect the streams shown in the People tab.
3. **Document assumptions:** Ensure every major volume expectation has a documented assumption so changes can be tracked.
4. **Align schedule:** Use the accepted forecast to guide long-term block scheduling in the Production planning views.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- **Unstated assumptions:** Creating a forecast based on adding a second shift, but not writing that assumption down.
- **Ignoring history:** Forecasting a 50% increase in output without changing any underlying processes, despite historical data showing current limits.

## Quick example
| Period | Expected Volume | Key Assumption / Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2026 | 45 Units | Assumes Unit 6 expansion completes in July. |
| Q4 2026 | 60 Units | Dependent on successful hiring of 3 new technicians. |
